/RATINGS

How I score things in the garden. One number, out of five.

THE SCALE

Every book, film, show, game, and album in the garden gets a single number. I rate on how much I got out of the thing, not on how well made it is - a flawed film I think about for a week beats a competent one I forget by morning.

rating label what it means
(1/5) terrible Bad enough that I stopped, or wish I had. Most of these are DNFs.
(2/5) below average I finished it, but it did not earn the time. Something in here does not work.
(3/5) average Fine. Okay. Does what it set out to do and little more. No regrets, no urgency.
(4/5) above average Clearly better than most. I would recommend it without hedging.
(5/5) great A favorite. It goes on the favorites shelf at the top of its page and stays there.

Half steps are allowed when I genuinely cannot pick a side. Anything I have logged but not yet scored shows as (?/5).

THE COLORS

Each step has its own color, so a wall of covers reads at a glance without stopping to parse the numbers. The scale runs warm to cool: warm for the things that did not land, green for the ones that did.

--rating-terrible

#e86b6b

--rating-below-average

#d9a441

--rating-good

#bdbd7f

--rating-above-average

#a9c94f

--rating-great

#7ec98f

Color is never the only signal - the number is always there next to it. In high contrast mode the five tokens swap for brighter values, and an unrated entry falls back to --text-secondary.

IN MARKUP

One span, two classes. The modifier carries the color through a --rating-color custom property, so the base class is all that needs to match if I ever swap the palette. 

<span class="rating rating--great">(5/5)</span>

Modifiers, 1 to 5: rating--terrible, rating--below-average, rating--good, rating--above-average, rating--great. Drop the modifier for an unrated entry.
